    ADR 003: the detector that failed, and why the explanation was wrong¶

    Status: accepted, after being rewritten Date: 2026-10-21

    What this decision was originally¶

    bowley-skew scores beacons by one minus the absolute Bowley skewness of their intervals. In the first sweep it reached the target detection rate nowhere: not at any number of intervals, not at any jitter, including no jitter at all.

    The decision was to keep it anyway and publish the row of failures, and the argument was this:

    The failure was predicted and then observed. blind_to was written from the mathematics before the sweep ran, and the sweep found what it said. That is the strongest available evidence that the declaration requirement is doing real work rather than generating plausible prose.

    That argument was wrong, and the way it was wrong is worth more than the decision it was defending.

    What was actually happening¶

    Bowley skewness is (Q3 + Q1 - 2*Q2) / (Q3 - Q1). The denominator is zero when every interval in the middle half is identical, which is what a perfect beacon looks like, so the code guarded it:

    if spread <= 0:
    return 1.0

    That guard catches only exact floating point equality. A perfect beacon's intervals are not exactly equal. They are produced by np.diff(np.cumsum(sleeps)), and the running sum accumulates rounding error whenever the period is not representable in binary, which is almost always. A perfect beacon with a period of 2047.3771254706232 seconds has an interquartile spread of 4.5e-13 seconds, which is not zero, so the skewness was computed as a ratio of two quantities that were both pure rounding noise.

    The result was a scale free statistic applied to numerical dust. A perfect beacon scored 0.0. Across periods drawn the way the benchmark draws them, 39 percent of perfect beacons scored below 0.5 and only 45 percent scored 1.0. The one percent false positive threshold for this detector sits near 0.99, so most perfect beacons were misses before any jitter was applied at all.

    The fix is a relative guard rather than an exact one: treat a spread that is negligible against the median as degenerate. After it, every one of 4000 perfect beacons scores 1.0, and the detector reaches the target at low jitter.

    Why the wrong explanation survived¶

    The declared blindness was real. Uniform jitter is nearly skew free in both models, so bowley-skew genuinely does struggle as jitter rises, and it genuinely is a shape test rather than a regularity test.

    So when the sweep produced a row of failures, there was a true statement available that explained it, and the explanation was flattering: a declaration written before the measurement had predicted the result. It made the blind_to requirement look like it was doing real work. Nothing about the output contradicted it.

    What it could not explain, and what nobody asked, is why the detector failed at zero jitter, where its declared blindness does not apply at all. That column was right there in the published table, reading never, and the story had no room for it.

    An independent review of the source found the guard. It was not found by rerunning the benchmark, by adding seeds, or by reading the results, because all of those were consistent with the wrong explanation.

    Decision¶

    Keep bowley-skew, on an argument that no longer depends on the failure:

    • It is a different kind of statistic from the other four, a shape test rather than a dispersion or spectral one, and the comparison is more informative with it than without it.
    • Its real blindness, that uniform jitter is nearly skew free, is a measured result now rather than an unfalsifiable one, because it can be seen degrading from a correct score at zero jitter instead of starting at zero.

    And record the methodological consequence, which applies to the whole project:

    A result that confirms a declaration is not evidence that the declaration was doing work. It is the case where checking is most needed and least likely, because the explanation is already there and it is satisfying. The discipline that caught this was not internal; it was handing the code to a reader who had not been told what to expect.

    Consequences¶

    • The published floors changed. Every number in docs/metrics.json and everything generated from it was re-measured after the fix.
    • A test pins a perfect beacon at 1.0 across 300 randomly drawn periods, so this class of bug cannot return quietly.
    • The same review found three other defects of the same shape, where a true statement was standing in front of a bug. They are in the commit history with the same treatment.
    • docs/limitations.md gains the general version: the results here have been reviewed from the outside once, and the things that survive that are not the same as the things that survive rerunning your own benchmark.
